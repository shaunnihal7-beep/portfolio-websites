La Filosofia

Restraint, before it
was a virtue.

“We don't cook a dish until there's nothing left to add. We cook until there's nothing left to take away.” — Elio Bassano, Executive Chef

MARMO occupies the old stables of Palazzo Serlupi, a five-minute walk from Piazza Navona, where the vaulted stone ceilings still carry the tool marks of the masons who cut them in 1584. Chef Elio Bassano trained in three-Michelin-starred kitchens across Piedmont and Liguria before returning to Rome with a single house rule: every plate must be reducible to three ingredients, no more.

The result looks sparse and tastes complete — Gragnano pasta dressed in nothing but three-year pecorino and cracked pepper, a turbot that touches the pan for ninety seconds, a risotto finished tableside with black winter truffle from the hills above Norcia. Nothing arrives to impress. Everything arrives because it has to.

Gli Spazi

Three rooms,
one stone.

Sala Marmo seats twenty-eight beneath the original stable vaults. La Cantina holds four thousand labels two floors below street level. Il Cortile, open only from May to September, seats twelve beneath a single lemon tree.
